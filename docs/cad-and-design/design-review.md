# Design review checklist

A design review should reveal constraints and tradeoffs, not reward whoever presents the best-looking rendering.

## Five questions

Define measurable requirements: speed, mass, reach, load, repeatability, serviceability, cost. Link each requirement to a test or calculation.

Check available stock, lead times, machining access, printer constraints, assembly order, and tolerances.

Locate fasteners and wear parts. Check whether components can be replaced without removing unrelated assemblies.

Identify likely jams, impacts, cable pinch points, bending, misalignment, and software/actuation failure modes.

Ask for calculations, fit checks, prototypes, test data, or documented prior experience. Distinguish assumption from measurement.

## One-page decision record

| Field              | Record                            |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Design goal        | What problem are we solving?      |
| Options considered | What alternatives were evaluated? |
| Decision           | What did we choose?               |
| Evidence           | What supports this choice?        |
| Open risks         | What still needs validation?      |
| Next review        | Who will follow up, and when?     |

**Review outcome:** approved for prototype, revise and resubmit, or stop and rethink. A rejection with useful reasons is progress.

## Bring evidence to the meeting

Use [FRCDesign’s design handbook](https://www.frcdesign.org/design-handbook/) for deeper design references and [GM0 mechanisms](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/) for FTC tradeoffs.

**A useful review packet:** one dimensioned interface view, one calculation or prototype observation, the manufacturing plan, and the most consequential unknown. Assign the follow-up test and owner before leaving.
