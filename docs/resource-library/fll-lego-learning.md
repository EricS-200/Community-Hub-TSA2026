# FLL & LEGO learning

**Begin with your program and current challenge materials.** [FIRST LEGO League](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/fll/get-started), [FIRST technical resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/fll/technical-resources), and [LEGO Education season materials](https://education.lego.com/en-us/first-lego-league/season-materials/) provide the official starting points. Check the division and approved technology before choosing lessons.

## A learning path with visible progress

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="First sessions" %}
[Getting started with SPIKE Prime](https://education.lego.com/en-us/start/spike-prime/) and [LEGO’s video tutorials](https://education.lego.com/en-us/product-resources/spike-prime/teacher-resources/video-tutorials/) introduce the hardware and software. Watch a short segment, then let students predict what changing one block will do.

**Keep:** a student explanation and a small working program, not a coach-written mission solution.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Sensors & repeatability" %}
[Prime Lessons](https://primelessons.org/en/Lessons.html), by the creators of EV3Lessons, supplies organized Scratch lessons; its [Python path](https://primelessons.org/en/PyLessons.html) supports students ready for text programming. Choose the lesson matching your SPIKE software generation.

**Try:** compare a time-based movement with a sensor-based stop under repeatable conditions. Log starting alignment, surface and outcome across several trials. Let students explain the variation before changing the design.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Innovation project" %}
Use the current official challenge guide to define the research problem. Ask a relevant community member about their needs, record sources and feedback, then revise an idea. The team should be able to explain what changed because of that feedback.

**Keep:** a source log, interview questions, a prototype or concept, and a short student explanation of the revision. Protect participants' personal information in public materials.
{% endtab %}
{% endtabs %}

<summary>A student-led review that takes five minutes</summary>

Ask each student to point to one contribution, explain one failed idea and name the next test. Rotate driving, building, programming and explaining. Record what students said in their own words. Keep robot-game scoring, research and Core Values visible throughout the season.

</details>
